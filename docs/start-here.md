# Start here: your first useful AI task

You do not need to know how to code, and you do not need to sign up for ten products. Begin with a task you already understand and can check yourself.

## Exercise: write a customer email

Imagine you run a small online shop. Someone asks when their order will arrive. The order has shipped, but you do not know the delivery date.

1. Open an [AI assistant](../README.md#ai-assistants) you are permitted to use.
2. Copy the sample request below. The shop and order are fictional.
3. Read the draft. Does it promise an arrival date or refund you did not approve?
4. Make any edits you need. Decide whether the whole process, including checking, was useful.

### Request to copy

> I run a small online shop. A customer asks when order EXAMPLE-123 will arrive. I only know that it has shipped. Draft a friendly email of fewer than 100 words. Do not invent a delivery date, tracking number, refund or company policy. If something is unknown, say so clearly.

**A good result:** It acknowledges the question, says the order has shipped and avoids a delivery promise. It does not invent a tracking link. You are responsible for approving the final reply.

## A simple formula for future requests

- **Task:** What you want, such as an email, summary or action list.
- **Context:** Facts the tool needs and you are allowed to share.
- **Boundaries:** What must not be guessed, promised or changed.
- **Review:** What you will check before putting the result to use.

For example: "Turn these approved meeting notes into five action items. Only use the notes. If a person or date isn't specified, write 'not specified'."

## Five habits worth keeping

- **Protect private data.** Do not paste passwords, customer records or confidential documents into unapproved tools.
- **Check facts.** Text can sound convincing while names, numbers or dates are wrong.
- **Keep people in control.** Important financial, customer and employment decisions need appropriate human review.
- **Begin with drafts.** Start with work you can correct before exploring automatic actions.
- **Count total effort.** Include the time you spend verifying, revising and paying for a tool.
