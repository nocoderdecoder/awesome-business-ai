# Everyday business uses for AI

These are small starting points for people who run businesses or work on business teams. They describe **possible tasks**, not promises of results.

## Browse by your work

| If you work in... | Try this first | Check before using the result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing | Draft different versions of a product announcement | Product facts, claims, brand voice |
| Sales | Draft questions for a first customer meeting | Public sources and unsupported assumptions |
| Customer service | Write a first draft of a response to a common issue | Policy, promises, escalation |
| Operations | Turn approved meeting notes into a task list | Owners, dates and missing decisions |
| Finance | Explain a known budget variance in plain English | All amounts, periods and calculations |
| Hiring | Draft a neutral job description | Real requirements, fairness and approval |
| Online retail | Rewrite a product description | Product specifications, prices and policies |
| Management | Summarize an approved plan for a weekly update | Completed work versus proposed work |
| Employee training | Make an FAQ from approved materials | Accuracy and current policies |
| Small business | List repetitive administrative tasks worth reviewing | Time, cost and risk |
| International work | Translate a short business email | Names, commercial terms and local meaning |
| Meetings | Make action items from a permitted recording | Consent, owners and due dates |

## Four example requests you can adapt

### Marketing: product announcement

> I sell reusable notebooks. Write three short product-announcement drafts for a general audience. Use only these verified product facts: [add facts]. Do not add discounts, customer testimonials, prices or environmental claims.

**Check:** Does every statement match the product? Select and edit one draft before publishing.

### Sales: preparing for a first meeting

> Using only the public company description below, suggest five discovery questions for a first customer call. Separate known facts from hypotheses. Do not invent the company's goals or challenges.

**Check:** Verify any facts independently. Even when you don't know an answer, a good discovery question can help you learn it.

Read the [sales research guide](playbooks/sales-research.md) for a deeper example.

### Customer service: responding to a late delivery

> A customer asks why a package is late. Our only confirmed information is that it has shipped. We have no delivery estimate. Write a polite reply that does not promise arrival, compensation or a refund.

**Check:** Confirm the wording follows actual company policies and that a person approves it.

Read the [customer support guide](playbooks/support-triage.md) for a deeper example.

### Operations: meeting follow-up

> Turn these approved meeting notes into a table with decision, action, owner and due date. If the notes do not identify an owner or date, write "not specified". Do not invent agreements.

**Check:** Confirm assignments with the team before publishing.

## Which task should you choose?

A good first task is **frequent, easy to check, low risk and somewhat repetitive**. Avoid starting with work where a mistake could cause an unauthorized payment, harmful advice, unfair hiring outcome or irreversible customer action.

Compare the time you spend from start to final approved output, not only the time it takes a tool to draft something. If it is not helping, change the task or stick with the existing process.
